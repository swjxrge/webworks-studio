# Week 6 — UX Evaluation Brief
## Hill Country Trail Guide

**Primary User:** Maya Torres  
**Primary Task:** Choose a beginner-appropriate Saturday hike that can be completed in about three hours or less.

---

## 1. Task Walkthrough
Briefly describe what Maya would try to do first, what she would look for, and where she might hesitate.

I think Maya would try to do jinper creek loop first. its 4.8 miles and a easy hike and it also is scenic and has an overlook. The details did mention rocky crossings and caution after rain, and there's no estimated completion time.

---

## 2. Five UX Findings

Document **exactly five meaningful findings**.

### Finding 1
**Observation:**  
Trails do not include estimated hiking times.

**Evidence:**  
Juniper Creek Loop lists “4.8 mi · 620 ft,” but neither its card nor details gives a duration.

**User Impact:**  
Maya cannot confirm whether she can finish within three hours.

**Principle:**  
Provide information relevant to the user’s task.
**Priority:**  High

**Recommendation:**  
Provide estimated completion-time ranges that account for beginner pacing.

### Finding 2
**Observation:**  
Difficulty labels do not explain the experience or effort required.

**Evidence:**  
Juniper is labeled “Easy,” but its details mention rocky crossings and caution after rain without explaining how these affect beginner suitability.

**User Impact:**  
Maya could select a trail that exceeds her comfort level.

**Principle:**  
Match between the system and the real world; plain language.

**Priority:**  
High

**Recommendation:**  
Explain difficulty through clear descriptions of terrain, climbing, and required experience.

### Finding 3
**Observation:**  
Categories look selectable but cannot narrow the trail list.

**Evidence:**  
“Easy,” “Moderate,” and other categories are plain text elements, despite the instruction “Choose a category above to narrow the list.” They have no filtering behavior or keyboard focusability.

**User Impact:**  
Maya cannot filter for beginner-friendly options, and keyboard users cannot access these apparent controls.

**Principle:**  
Clear interaction expectations; keyboard accessibility.
**Priority:**  
High

**Recommendation:** 
Make filtering functional and keyboard accessible, with clear feedback about selections and results. 

### Finding 4
**Observation:** 
Access information is vague, and planning links do not provide useful destinations.

**Evidence:**  
Juniper’s description says fees and gate hours may change. “Plan your hike” and “Conditions” link to #, rather than relevant planning information.

**User Impact:**  
Maya must search elsewhere to confirm whether her Saturday visit is practical.

**Principle:** 
Support task completion through actionable information and meaningful navigation.

**Priority:**  High
**Recommendation:**  
Provide clear access information and working links to current park rules, fees, hours, and conditions.

### Finding 5
**Observation:** 
Terrain warnings appear below the trail cards in separate descriptions.

**Evidence:**  
Juniper’s rocky-crossing warning appears in its detail section after all three cards. The phone layout stacks the cards before those details.

**User Impact:** 
 Maya must move between summaries and details and remember information while comparing trails.

**Principle:**  
Recognition rather than recall; clear information hierarchy.

**Priority:** 
Medium

**Recommendation:**  
Make essential suitability information easy to scan and compare alongside each trail’s summary.

---

## 3. Top Three Priorities
Identify the three findings that should move forward into Week 7.

1. Missing completion times: Maya needs to know whether a hike fits her three-hour limit. Estimated durations would help her choose without guessing from distance alone.

2. Unclear difficulty ratings: Maya needs to understand what “Easy” means for a beginner. Clear explanations of terrain and effort would help her judge whether Juniper fits her comfort level.

3. Nonfunctional filters: Maya cannot narrow the list as the interface promises. Working, accessible filters would help her find suitable options more efficiently.

For each, briefly explain why it matters to Maya's primary task.
